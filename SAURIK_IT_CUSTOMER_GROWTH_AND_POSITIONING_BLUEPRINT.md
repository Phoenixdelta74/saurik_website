# SAURIK IT Customer Growth and Positioning Blueprint

**Version:** 1.0  
**Prepared:** 14 September 2026  
**Primary objective:** Win the first repeatable set of paying customers in Tripura, expand across Northeast India, and create a credible path to international business.  
**Audience:** Founder, marketing/sales operator, and implementation agent working on the SAURIK IT website and growth system.

---

## 1. Executive decision

SAURIK IT should not enter the market as a generic company that offers every kind of software, AI, website, CCTV, computer, and server service with equal emphasis. That positioning is too broad for a new company without an extensive public portfolio.

The recommended market position is:

> **SAURIK IT helps growing organizations replace unverifiable field activity, fragmented spreadsheets, and repetitive operational work with practical software, applied AI, and dependable IT infrastructure.**

Use three layers:

1. **Primary wedge:** Field distribution and mobile operations, led by Saurik Track.
2. **Secondary service:** Custom operational software, data analytics, and carefully bounded AI workflow automation.
3. **Regional supporting service:** CCTV, computers, and server installation/service for Tripura and nearby serviceable areas.

Do not market generic ERP/CRM implementation, printers, networking, or annual maintenance contracts unless the founder explicitly confirms those offerings. Saurik Track is the product-specific mobile ERP exception.

### Why this wedge is recommended

- It solves observable operational problems: unverifiable visits, mobile inventory movement, manual reporting, disconnected spreadsheets, delayed reconciliation, and weak audit trails.
- It provides a reason to choose SAURIK IT beyond "we build websites and apps."
- It can begin locally through in-person discovery and later be sold remotely.
- Tripura's official industrial sources identify rubber, tea, bamboo, agro-horticulture, food processing, manufacturing, tourism, healthcare, and related industrial clusters. Many such organizations have field, dispatch, stock, inspection, or multi-location workflows.
- It creates reusable product knowledge while still allowing paid custom work around integrations and operational reporting.

This remains a market hypothesis until validated through qualified customer conversations and paid pilots.

## 2. Goals and boundaries

### 2.1 Twelve-month goals

- Secure the first **5 paying reference customers**.
- Produce at least **3 approved, evidence-backed case studies**.
- Establish a repeatable acquisition channel in Tripura and one neighboring Northeast market.
- Achieve a consistent rate of **8-12 qualified discovery conversations per month**.
- Test one international beachhead, initially the UAE, only after at least two credible case studies exist.
- Make the website technically indexable and locally relevant without turning it into a collection of low-value location pages.

### 2.2 First 90-day goals

- Interview 30 qualified business operators.
- Demonstrate Saurik Track or a workflow prototype to at least 12 prospects.
- Start 3-5 controlled design-partner pilots.
- Convert at least 1-2 pilots into paid engagements.
- Publish the minimum proof package: founder identity, company facts, real product demonstration, security/privacy explanation, and one approved customer story.

These are operating targets, not guarantees.

### 2.3 Non-goals

- Do not chase national or global brand awareness before proving one repeatable offer.
- Do not compete on "cheapest website in Tripura."
- Do not offer free custom development to build a portfolio.
- Do not publish fabricated testimonials, client logos, metrics, certifications, partnerships, or product claims.
- Do not mass-generate SEO pages for every city or service.
- Do not send bulk WhatsApp messages or unpersonalized email blasts.

## 3. Positioning architecture

### 3.1 Master brand

**Category:** Operational technology partner  
**Audience:** Growing businesses and institutions with field, stock, data, workflow, or infrastructure complexity  
**Promise:** Turn operational uncertainty and manual work into visible, controlled workflows  
**Mechanism:** Purpose-built software, applied AI with human review, and locally supported infrastructure  
**Personality:** Deliberate, practical, accountable, clear, technically strong, and never exaggerated

### 3.2 Regional positioning: Tripura and Northeast India

> **For growing organizations in Tripura and Northeast India, SAURIK IT is an operational technology partner that combines software, applied AI, and IT infrastructure around the way the organization actually works.**

Regional proof priorities:

- Local availability and accountable support
- Understanding of low-connectivity and distributed operating conditions
- Clear scope and handover
- Business-owner access to the technical team
- Software and physical infrastructure coordination where required

### 3.3 Global positioning

> **For international SMEs and agencies that need a focused delivery partner, SAURIK IT designs and builds operational web applications, data workflows, and human-reviewed AI automation with direct access to the people doing the work.**

Global offers must be software-only unless service delivery in another country is explicitly established. Do not present CCTV, computer sales, or server installation as globally available.

### 3.4 Product positioning: Saurik Track

> **Saurik Track helps distribution and service teams verify field activity, manage mobile stock, and replace end-of-day spreadsheet and WhatsApp reporting with one auditable operational workflow.**

Lead with outcomes, not surveillance language. Employee transparency, consent, purpose limitation, access control, retention, and review processes must be part of the product story.

### 3.5 Recommended homepage message

**Eyebrow:** SOFTWARE, AI AND IT INFRASTRUCTURE FROM TRIPURA  
**H1:** Make field work, stock movement, and operational decisions easier to verify.  
**Supporting copy:** SAURIK IT builds practical software, applied AI workflows, and dependable IT infrastructure for growing organizations in Tripura, Northeast India, and selected global markets.  
**Primary CTA:** Discuss an operational problem  
**Secondary CTA:** Explore Saurik Track

Avoid presenting the company as globally established until international evidence exists. "Available for selected international projects" is safer and more credible.

## 4. Use-case validation portfolio

Do not select a permanent niche only from desk research. Run three bounded tests and let customer evidence decide.

### Test A: Field distribution and mobile inventory

**Priority:** Highest  
**Product:** Saurik Track  
**Ideal problem:** A company manages field representatives, vehicles, deliveries, visits, or stock outside the main office using calls, WhatsApp, paper, or spreadsheets.

Questions to validate:

1. How many field representatives, vehicles, or mobile stock locations are active?
2. How are attendance and visits verified today?
3. How is stock transferred between warehouse, van, rep, and customer?
4. How many hours are spent reconciling reports?
5. What errors, disputes, stock differences, or missed visits occur?
6. What software is already used, and what must be preserved?
7. Who approves a pilot and who will use it daily?

Pilot success measures:

- Reduction in manual reconciliation time
- Percentage of planned visits with usable records
- Stock-transfer discrepancy rate
- User adoption rate
- Number and severity of support incidents
- Manager time saved, based on an agreed measurement method

### Test B: Operational workflow replacement

**Priority:** High  
**Service:** Custom web application and mobile workflow  
**Ideal problem:** A business-critical process is split across spreadsheets, paper, calls, and WhatsApp.

Candidate workflows:

- Quotations and approvals
- Dealer or distributor order capture
- Inventory and dispatch visibility
- Service requests and technician visits
- Inspection and compliance evidence
- Purchase request approval
- Multi-branch reporting
- Customer onboarding and document collection

Pilot format:

1. One paid discovery workshop
2. One workflow map
3. One prioritized prototype
4. One limited-user implementation
5. Evidence review before expanding scope

### Test C: Applied AI and data workflow

**Priority:** Medium  
**Service:** Data analytics, forecasting, retrieval, or human-reviewed workflow automation  
**Ideal problem:** Staff repeatedly search documents, prepare the same draft, combine reports, or make recurring decisions from scattered data.

Candidate use cases:

- Internal policy or document knowledge assistant
- Tender and specification summarization with source references
- Sales or inventory forecasting after data-quality assessment
- Repetitive proposal or quotation drafting with mandatory review
- Support-ticket triage
- Management reporting from approved data sources

AI projects must include data boundaries, evaluation examples, failure behavior, permission limits, human review, retention choices, and documented limitations.

### Validation decision after six weeks

Score each test from 1-5 on:

- Frequency of the problem
- Financial or operational consequence
- Buyer urgency
- Ability to reach the decision-maker
- Ability to demonstrate value within 30 days
- Reusability across customers
- Willingness to pay
- Delivery risk

Continue only the highest-scoring one or two motions. Pause the others rather than trying to sell everything.

## 5. Ideal customer profiles

### ICP 1: Distributor with field representatives or delivery vehicles

**Geography:** Tripura first; Assam and Meghalaya next; India-wide after proof  
**Size signal:** 5-100 field users, multiple routes, or mobile inventory  
**Buyer:** Owner, director, operations head, sales head, distribution manager  
**Current behavior:** WhatsApp photos, spreadsheet attendance, phone calls, handwritten stock transfer, separate billing system  
**Trigger:** Stock loss, visit disputes, expansion, new branch, audit concern, delayed reporting  
**Offer:** 14-30 day Saurik Track design-partner pilot  
**Disqualifier:** One-person operation, no repeated field process, unwilling to involve actual users, or request for covert employee surveillance

### ICP 2: Local manufacturer or processor

**Segments:** Food and beverage, rubber, tea, bamboo, packaging, feed, small industrial units  
**Buyer:** Owner, plant head, operations manager, commercial manager  
**Problem:** Dispatch, stock, quality checks, dealer orders, maintenance or inspection records are fragmented  
**Offer:** Operational workflow diagnostic followed by one limited implementation  
**Expansion:** Dashboards, forecasting, mobile inspection, distributor portal

### ICP 3: Multi-location service organization

**Segments:** Clinics, diagnostic centers, hotels, educational institutions, facilities/service teams  
**Buyer:** Founder, administrator, operations head  
**Problem:** Requests, visits, assets, approvals, schedules, or documents are managed manually  
**Offer:** Workflow map and small portal/mobile solution  
**Warning:** Healthcare or employee data requires enhanced privacy and access-control design.

### ICP 4: Documentation-heavy SME

**Segments:** Professional services, contractors, exporters, tender participants, training organizations  
**Buyer:** Founder, operations lead, finance/administration lead  
**Problem:** Staff repeatedly search documents, prepare submissions, or consolidate reports  
**Offer:** Human-reviewed document/data workflow pilot  
**Disqualifier:** Prospect expects unsupervised AI accuracy or wants sensitive information placed into an unapproved service.

### ICP 5: International digital agency or SME

**First test market:** UAE  
**Later markets:** UK and Singapore after proof  
**Buyer:** Agency founder, product owner, operations director, SME founder  
**Problem:** Needs a reliable delivery pod for operational applications, integrations, data tools, or carefully scoped AI workflows  
**Offer:** Fixed-scope discovery and delivery sprint with direct founder/technical access  
**Differentiator:** Clear scope, documented handover, practical communication, and no account-management layer  
**Disqualifier:** Unpaid test projects, vague staff-augmentation requests, unrealistic deadlines, or refusal to agree on acceptance criteria

## 6. Market sequence

### Wave 1: Tripura

Focus on founder-led discovery and high-trust local selling.

Priority sectors:

1. Pharmaceutical, FMCG, building-material, agricultural-input, and consumer-goods distribution
2. Food processing and packaged products
3. Rubber, tea, bamboo, packaging, and related industrial units
4. Vehicle dealers, service fleets, and logistics operators
5. Multi-location clinics, diagnostic centers, hotels, and educational organizations
6. Local government suppliers and contractors, subject to procurement eligibility

Tripura's 2024 industrial investment policy identifies rubber, bamboo, agro-horticulture, agar, tea, food processing, and other manufacturing opportunities. The state factory and industry datasets provide useful account-research seeds, but each organization's current status and contact channel must be verified before outreach.

### Wave 2: Northeast India

Start with **Assam**, then **Meghalaya**, because they offer larger reachable business networks and relatively practical travel/relationship paths from Tripura. Expand to other Northeast states only when a repeatable offer and support model exist.

Priority regional themes:

- Tea and agricultural supply chains
- Distribution and field sales
- Tourism and hospitality operations
- Healthcare and education workflows
- Handloom, handicraft, producer groups, and market-linkage operations
- Contractors and service fleets

Do not create eight nearly identical state pages. Create substantive industry/use-case content, then add a regional page with real delivery availability.

### Wave 3: Global

The UAE is the recommended first international experiment because of its large SME base, policy emphasis on digital transformation, time-zone compatibility, Indian business networks, and strong distribution, trading, services, hospitality, and logistics sectors.

Global sequence:

1. Obtain two or more credible Indian case studies.
2. Create an English global services page and one concise capability deck.
3. Build a list of 50 UAE SMEs or agencies matching one specific problem.
4. Run personalized founder-to-founder outreach.
5. Offer a paid, fixed-scope discovery or integration sprint.
6. Deliver one referenceable international project.
7. Test UK or Singapore only after the offer, commercial process, data handling, and support hours are repeatable.

## 8. Lead qualification and scoring

Create a lightweight CRM or spreadsheet with these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Account name | Organization being researched |
| Sector | Distribution, manufacturing, healthcare, etc. |
| Geography | City, state, country |
| Public source URL | Where the account was found |
| Decision-maker role | Role, not assumed personal identity |
| Current workflow | Tools and process presently used |
| Pain hypothesis | Specific problem SAURIK IT may solve |
| Evidence | Public or discovery-call evidence supporting the hypothesis |
| User/fleet/location count | Size indicator |
| Trigger event | Expansion, hiring, new branch, audit, product launch, etc. |
| Offer fit | Track, custom workflow, AI/data, hardware |
| Lead score | 0-100 |
| Contact status | Researching, contacted, replied, discovery, pilot, won, lost |
| Next action and date | One concrete follow-up |
| Consent/opt-out note | Outreach compliance record |
| Loss reason | Price, timing, no pain, trust, product gap, etc. |

### Lead score

Score each factor 0-5 and multiply by the weight:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Repeated operational pain | 5 |
| Financial or compliance consequence | 4 |
| Field users, vehicles, stock, or multiple locations | 4 |
| Decision-maker is reachable | 3 |
| Urgent trigger exists | 2 |
| Existing workflow can be measured | 2 |

Maximum raw weighted score is 100. Prioritize accounts scoring 65 or higher. Do not contact an account merely because it appears in a directory; first create a credible account-specific pain hypothesis.

## 9. Offer ladder

### Entry offer: Operational Workflow Diagnostic

**Format:** 30-minute qualification conversation followed by a short written problem summary  
**Promise:** Clarify whether the problem deserves software, process improvement, or no project  
**Output:** Current workflow, pain, baseline metric, users, constraints, next recommendation  
**Price:** Founder decision; the first conversation may be free, but detailed process mapping should be paid

### Pilot offer: Design Partner Pilot

**Format:** 14-30 days, limited users and one measurable workflow  
**Required agreement:** Scope, data responsibilities, success metrics, support boundary, price, termination, and permission for any future case study  
**Commercial rule:** A discount may be exchanged for structured feedback and case-study permission. Do not exchange the entire implementation for a testimonial.

### Core offer: Operational System Build

**Format:** Discovery, prototype, staged delivery, verification, handover, optional support  
**Rule:** Preserve and integrate existing workflows when practical; do not require a complete system replacement unless evidence supports it.

### Global entry offer: Fixed-Scope Delivery Sprint

**Format:** One clearly defined workflow, integration, dashboard, or AI evaluation prototype  
**Commercial rule:** Paid discovery or milestone-based fixed scope; written acceptance criteria; repository and handover terms agreed before delivery

### Objection handling

| Objection | Response direction |
|---|---|
| "You are a new company." | Agree directly. Show the working product, founder capability, documented process, and offer a controlled pilot with measurable exit criteria. |
| "We already use spreadsheets/WhatsApp." | Do not criticize the tools. Identify where the handoffs, verification, reconciliation, or history break down. Improve only that part first. |
| "Another company is cheaper." | Compare scope, ownership, support, security, data handling, acceptance criteria, and handover—not feature count alone. |
| "We do not want employee surveillance." | Explain purpose limitation, visible employee disclosure, access controls, retention, review, and a workflow designed around legitimate operational needs. |
| "AI makes mistakes." | Agree. Show evaluation, source use, human review, permission limits, and failure behavior. Do not promise autonomous accuracy. |
| "Can you build everything?" | Narrow the first outcome and explain what is explicitly outside the initial scope. |

## 11. Outreach system

### 11.1 Weekly founder-led cadence

- Research 25-40 accounts.
- Select 15-20 accounts with a specific, evidence-backed pain hypothesis.
- Send 10-15 personalized emails or LinkedIn messages.
- Make 5-10 respectful local business calls or introductions.
- Ask existing professional contacts for 3 warm introductions.
- Hold 2-4 discovery conversations.
- Publish one useful demonstration, teardown, checklist, or founder insight.
- Review replies, objections, and loss reasons every Friday.

Quality is more important than volume during the first 90 days.

### 11.2 Local outreach message

**Subject:** A question about your field/dispatch workflow

Hello {{Name}},

I run SAURIK IT in Tripura. I noticed that {{specific public observation}}. Companies with similar field, delivery, or stock operations often spend significant time reconciling WhatsApp updates and spreadsheets.

I am speaking with a small number of local operators to understand how this is handled in practice. If this is relevant to {{Company}}, could I ask you three questions in a 20-minute conversation? I will share a short workflow summary afterwards, even if software is not the right answer.

Regards,  
{{Founder name}}  
SAURIK IT

### 11.3 Global outreach message

**Subject:** One operational workflow at {{Company}}

Hello {{Name}},

I lead SAURIK IT, an India-based product and software team focused on operational web applications, field workflows, data tools, and carefully bounded AI automation.

I noticed {{specific observation}}. I have a hypothesis that {{specific workflow}} may still involve manual handoffs or repeated reconciliation. If that is accurate, I can map the workflow and propose a small fixed-scope test rather than a broad transformation project.

Would a short conversation next week be useful?

Regards,  
{{Founder name}}

### 11.4 Follow-up sequence

1. **Day 0:** Personalized message with one relevant observation.
2. **Day 4:** Share a useful checklist, demo, or question—not "just following up."
3. **Day 10:** Offer a specific 20-minute workflow review.
4. **Day 21:** Close the loop politely and provide an opt-out.

Stop after the final message unless the prospect responds. Respect all opt-outs immediately.

### 11.5 Discovery-call structure

1. Why did the prospect agree to speak now?
2. Walk through the current workflow step by step.
3. Where do delays, disagreements, duplication, or missing information occur?
4. What is the current cost in time, money, risk, or customer experience?
5. Who uses, approves, and maintains the process?
6. What systems must remain?
7. What would a successful 30-day test prove?
8. Agree on the next step or explicitly close the opportunity.

Do not demo before understanding the workflow unless the prospect requested a product demonstration.

## 16. Success metrics

### Weekly dashboard

| Metric | Initial target | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Accounts researched | 25-40 | Maintain pipeline quality |
| Qualified accounts selected | 15-20 | Avoid indiscriminate outreach |
| Personalized first contacts | 10-15 | Test message and ICP |
| Positive replies | 2-4 | Measure relevance |
| Discovery conversations | 2-4 | Learn and qualify |
| Demonstrations | 1-3 | Test solution fit |
| Pilot proposals | 1+ | Create commercial movement |
| Paying conversions | 1-2 per month initially | Validate the business |

### Funnel definitions

- **Researched account:** Publicly verified organization with a source link.
- **Qualified account:** Matches ICP and has a credible pain hypothesis.
- **Positive reply:** Expresses relevance or agrees to a conversation.
- **Qualified discovery:** Pain, consequence, buyer, timing, and next step are understood.
- **Pilot:** Written scope, users, data, success measures, price, and dates are agreed.
- **Won customer:** Payment or binding commercial agreement exists.

### Decision rules

- If 50 well-targeted contacts produce fewer than 3 positive replies, revise the audience or message.
- If discovery calls do not reveal recurring costly pain, change the use case.
- If demos attract interest but no paid pilot, examine trust, scope, onboarding, price, and buyer authority.
- If pilots do not produce measurable value, fix the product or abandon that segment before increasing marketing spend.

## 17. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Company appears too broad | Prospects cannot understand why to choose SAURIK IT | Lead with one problem and one CTA; keep other services secondary |
| New-company trust gap | Prospects prefer established competitors | Use working product, founder evidence, controlled pilots, transparent scope, and real case studies |
| Unsupported claims | Legal, reputational, and sales risk | Maintain a claim register; require evidence and owner approval before publication |
| Overbuilding before discovery | Time is spent on features nobody buys | Require interviews, pilot commitments, and measurable success criteria before expansion |
| Free-work trap | Activity without revenue | Charge for discovery and pilots; discount only for explicit design-partner value |
| Employee privacy concerns | Product rejection or harm | Build transparent use, consent/notice, access, retention, export, and complaint handling into Track |
| Low-quality outreach | Brand damage and opt-outs | Personalize, limit cadence, use business channels, and stop promptly |
| Premature international expansion | Diluted focus and delivery strain | Enter one country with one offer only after Indian proof exists |
| SEO spam | Search penalties and weak brand | Publish fewer, stronger pages with original evidence and human factual review |
